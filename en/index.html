About SOBEER

What is SOBEER?

SOBEER is an independent guide to the alcohol-free beer scene in Czechia. It maps the venues that treat alcohol-free beer not as a necessary evil but as a craft product in its own right.

For every venue you will find the address, opening hours, the specific alcohol-free beers split into draught and bottled, and whether there is a garden and food. The site is free, carries no advertising and is independent of the breweries.

How do venues get into SOBEER?

Venues are never added for money or to order. There is one criterion — they serve alcohol-free beer and they take it seriously.

Know a venue that is missing? Send us a tip through the Add a venue form. The database grows mainly thanks to tips from people like you.

Do you cover venues outside Prague?

Yes. We started in Prague and the database is still most detailed here, but we keep adding venues elsewhere — so far in the Central Bohemian Region, and it will go further.

The alcohol-free beer scene is developing across the whole country and we want to map all of it. If you know a good venue in your town, send us a tip through the Add a venue form. The database grows mainly thanks to tips from people who know their own neighbourhood.

Is the information about each venue up to date?

We verify the data and update it continuously, but what a venue pours changes — especially where seasonal specials rotate on the taps.

The information is therefore indicative only. If you are travelling for one particular beer, we recommend checking availability with the venue directly. Spotted a mistake? Write to us through the contact form.

Alcohol-free beer basics

Does alcohol-free beer contain alcohol?

Yes, but in trace amounts. Under Czech law a beer may be labelled alcohol-free if it contains no more than 0.5 % alcohol by volume. Most Czech alcohol-free beers sit between 0.3 and 0.5 %.

There are also beers declared at 0.0 % — typically international brands such as Bavaria, aiming at an absolute zero. For comparison, kefir, a ripe banana and yeast bread all contain similar amounts.

What is the difference between craft, traditional and Birell zeros?

On SOBEER we sort alcohol-free beers into three categories by origin and approach:

  • Craft — craft alcohol-free beers from small independent breweries. On SOBEER you will come across Vinohradský pivovar, Zichovec, Clock, Chříč, Sibeeria or Pivovar Uhříněves, among others — and the scene is far wider than that. These are often IPAs, sours or specials where the alcohol-free version is developed with the same care as the original.
  • Traditional — good alcohol-free beers, usually from established or international breweries, holding a classic beer profile. Bakalář, Bernard, Maisel, Budvar, Krušovice or Lobkowicz, among many others.
  • Birell — a category of its own. The pioneer of Czech alcohol-free beer, launched in 1992 and still the most widespread zero in the country. You will find it almost everywhere, which is both its strength and the reason we list it separately.

How is alcohol-free beer made?

There are two basic routes to staying under 0.5 % alcohol:

  • Never let the alcohol form — known as arrested or controlled fermentation. A special yeast strain is pitched into the wort which can process only simple sugars (glucose, fructose) but cannot digest maltose, the main sugar from the malt. Fermentation stops naturally before any measurable amount of alcohol is produced.
  • Remove the alcohol from finished beer — the brewery makes a full beer and then takes the alcohol out, either mechanically through a membrane (reverse osmosis) or by evaporation under vacuum at low temperature.

Most beers made by controlled fermentation have to be pasteurised — a lot of unfermented sugar stays in the beer, and without pasteurisation wild yeast in the can could finish the job and push the alcohol up.

Why does alcohol-free beer taste better than it used to?

Older methods cut fermentation short by force, leaving a sweet worty off-flavour that gave alcohol-free beer its long-standing reputation as an emergency option. Boiling the alcohol off, meanwhile, damaged the flavour through heat shock.

Today's technology gets around both problems. Special yeast strains let the beer ferment for the full standard period, while membrane filtration and vacuum distillation work cold. Brewers also compensate for the missing alcohol — which carries aroma in beer — with heavy dry hopping and recipe adjustments: higher mash temperatures, oat and rice flakes for body, biological acidification for freshness.

Which alcohol-free beers are worth trying

What is the best alcohol-free beer?

There is no single best alcohol-free beer — taste is subjective and it depends on what you want from it. One person is after a bitter lager with lunch, another a fruit bomb on a summer afternoon.

Among the beers with the strongest long-standing reputation are Vinohradská 12 Nealko and Vinohradská Hazy Galaxy IPA from Vinohradský pivovar, Clock Krystus from Potštejn, Chříč Balanc, the Drink range from Zichovec, Good News from Sibeeria, and Alois Šofér from Pivovar Uhříněves.

The best way to find yours is to taste several different styles side by side. That is exactly what SOBEER is for.

What is the best alcohol-free craft beer?

The choice is wide and keeps growing. In the craft alcohol-free category, these breweries come up most often:

  • Vinohradský pivovar — Vinohradská 12 Nealko and Hazy Galaxy IPA. Made by membrane filtration, which keeps them very close in flavour to the originals.
  • Clock — Krystus (an unfiltered alcohol-free IPA with Mosaic and Talus), Leviticus DH (a lager), Lazarus and Nana (fruit sours).
  • Zichovec — the Drink range: Drink and Drive (hazy IPA), Drink and Swim (passion fruit sour), Drink and Razz (raspberry).
  • Sibeeria — Good News NEIPA, a Gose with blood orange and rosemary, and a Czech Pilsner, plus Hop Water with neither calories nor alcohol.
  • Chříč — Balanc, an alcohol-free IPA with a pronounced spicy bitterness, plus a summer edition with lime and rosemary.

Which of them is best comes down to personal preference. They differ in style and in how they are made.

What is the best alcohol-free lager?

If you are after the classic Czech profile — bitterness, bite, malt body — there are several routes.

From the craft scene, the closest to a traditional Czech twelve is Vinohradská 12 Nealko, hopped with Saaz and Sládek. An interesting alternative is Clock Leviticus DH, a lager dry-hopped with American Amarillo, and Sibeeria Good News Czech Pilsner.

A special place belongs to Alois Šofér from Pivovar Uhříněves — one of the few alcohol-free beers on the market that is neither filtered nor pasteurised. Among the traditional brands, popular choices include Bernard, Bakalář or Budvar.

Is there such a thing as an alcohol-free IPA or sour?

Yes, and they are among the most interesting things the scene has to offer. Strong bitterness and acidity do an excellent job of masking the thinner body that often troubles alcohol-free beer.

Alcohol-free IPAs — Clock Krystus, Chříč Balanc, Zichovec Drink and Drive, Sibeeria Good News NEIPA, Vinohradská Hazy Galaxy or Maisel Urban IPA Alkoholfrei, for example.

Alcohol-free sours — Clock Lazarus (passion fruit, mango, peach) and Nana (raspberry), Zichovec Drink and Swim and Drink and Razz, or Sibeeria's Gose with blood orange and rosemary.

Wheat beers — Maisel's Weisse Alkoholfrei, Paulaner Weissbier 0.0 % or Bavaria 0.0 % Wit, for example.

How each brewery makes its zero

What technology does Vinohradský pivovar use?

Vinohradský pivovar is the only Czech craft brewery taking the route of membrane filtration, that is reverse osmosis. The process runs at their new site in Káraný.

First an entirely standard beer is brewed, fermented out and matured — so it has full body, natural bitterness and complex flavour. The finished beer is then forced under pressure along a semi-permeable membrane whose pores let only the smallest molecules through: water and alcohol. Hop oils, proteins and aromatic compounds are too large and stay behind as a thick beer concentrate.

The water removed is then returned to the concentrate — local groundwater naturally filtered through the gravel beds of the Jizera. The beer never undergoes heat shock, so it keeps the aroma and character of the original.

How does Zichovec make its alcohol-free beer?

The family brewery Zichovec relies on controlled fermentation with special yeast strains. The alcohol is never removed — it simply barely forms in the first place.

The yeast ferments the wort only very slightly and the alcohol level naturally stays under 0.5 %. Because most of the sugars remain untouched, Zichovec has to pasteurise the finished cans so that no stray wild yeast finishes the fermentation inside the packaging.

Zichovec's alcohol-free beers are easy to spot by names beginning with Drink — Drink and Drive, Drink and Shine, Drink and Swim, Drink and Razz, Drink and Grill, Drink and Be Real.

How is Clock Krystus made?

Brewery Clock in Potštejn uses, like Zichovec, controlled fermentation. The yeast processes only simple sugars, leaves the malt-derived maltose alone, and fermentation stops by itself below the legal limit.

Because a high proportion of malt sugars remains, the beer never tastes watery. Clock makes up for the missing alcohol with heavy dry hopping — Krystus is an unfiltered alcohol-free IPA hopped with Mosaic and Talus, which bring notes of tropical fruit, peach and citrus. Every batch is pasteurised at the end.

Besides Krystus, Clock also brews the alcohol-free lager Leviticus DH and the fruit sours Lazarus and Nana.

How is Chříč Balanc made?

Brewery Chříč in the Křivoklát area — a sheltered workshop housed in a baroque brewery — makes Balanc by controlled fermentation with special yeast strains, the same biological route taken by Zichovec and Clock.

The yeast consumes only simple sugars and then naturally stops working. Plenty of unfermented malt sugar stays in the beer, which gives Balanc full body and keeps it from tasting watery. The finished beer is pasteurised.

Balanc is a pale top-fermented alcohol-free IPA with a pronounced spicy bitterness and tropical fruit aroma. Besides the standard version there is a summer edition, Balanc Limeta & Rozmarýn.

What makes Alois Šofér from Pivovar Uhříněves unusual?

Šofér is one of the few alcohol-free beers on the market that is neither filtered nor pasteurised. It is packaged live, stays slightly hazy and keeps a full bitter character close to a classic Czech ten-degree lager.

The brewery uses controlled fermentation with a special yeast strain, but the rest of the process is purely traditional: floor-malted Pilsner malt and Saaz hops, which also dry-hop the beer directly in the lagering tank.

While other craft breweries experiment with sours and hazy IPAs, Pivovar Uhříněves sticks to the Czech lager line.

How are international zeros made — Maisel and Bavaria?

Both breweries use vacuum distillation. At normal pressure alcohol evaporates at 78 °C, which would ruin the beer. Under vacuum the boiling point drops to roughly 35–40 °C, so the beer never undergoes heat shock.

Maisel in Bayreuth, Bavaria, adds a step of its own: the dealcoholised batch is blended with a second wort whose fermentation was deliberately stopped at the very start. The result has hop aroma and malt body alike.

Bavaria in the Netherlands aims at an absolute 0.0 %. For its main beer styles it uses vacuum distillation; for malt drinks it uses its own patented bioreactor with immobilised yeast, which gives the wort beer character without producing any alcohol at all.

How is Birell made?

Birell is brewed at the Radegast brewery and is the pioneer of the whole category — it reached the Czech market back in 1992, when brewmaster Ivo Kaňák brought a special yeast strain back from Zurich.

It uses controlled fermentation. The yeast produces minimal alcohol while still creating the same flavour and aroma compounds as a classic lager fermentation. Fermentation runs the full standard 21 days with nothing cut short, so no sweet worty off-flavour remains.

It is brewed on the same equipment as Radegast, from Pilsner and caramel malt and Czech hops. The beer is pasteurised before packaging.

Where to find alcohol-free beer

Where can I find alcohol-free beer on tap in Prague?

Alcohol-free beer on tap stopped being a rarity a long time ago. You will find it in the list above — just set the How it is served filter to Draught, and add the Craft filter if you want craft breweries only.

The strongest coverage is in Vinohrady, Žižkov, Karlín, Holešovice and Dejvice. The map above shows venues near you — allow location access and it will centre on where you are.

Which Prague breweries make their own alcohol-free beer?

Vinohradský pivovar — Vinohradská 12 Nealko and Hazy Galaxy IPA, made by membrane filtration in Káraný. You can drink them at the brewery on Korunní and in plenty of other venues around the city.

Pivovar Uhříněves — Alois Šofér, an unfiltered and unpasteurised lager. On tap at the brewery restaurant and beyond it.

Sibeeria — a brewery in Vysočany with the Good News range and Hop Water hop waters.

Outside Prague itself, worth attention are Zichovec from the Slaný area, Chříč from the Křivoklát area and Clock from Potštejn.

Where can I buy alcohol-free craft beer in Prague?

The best selection of bottled and canned alcohol-free specials is in the bottle shops. The SOBEER list includes several where you can drink in or take away.

Set the filters to How it is served → Bottles & cans and Beer category → Craft. Stock in bottle shops turns over quickly, so it is always worth checking on a specific beer in advance.

About

Beer is a ritual, a flavour and an experience. We all know the moment when the first sip settles the soul. It would be a shame to miss out on that simply because you cannot or do not want to drink alcohol — and all the more so here in Czechia, the home of beer. The Czech beer scene is among the very best in the world, and it stopped being only about alcohol a long time ago.

SOBEER began as an independent initiative to support the growth of the alcohol-free beer scene in Czechia. It is a guide for anyone looking for an honest zero, mapping the venues that treat alcohol-free options not as a necessary evil but as a quality craft product.

We started in Prague and the database is most detailed here, but we keep reaching further — the alcohol-free scene is growing across the whole country and we want to map all of it. So we would like to ask you, the people who love beer in all its forms, to help us. If you know a venue in your town with a great alcohol-free selection that is missing here, let us know. Together we can help the alcohol-free beer culture earn its place in the sun.
